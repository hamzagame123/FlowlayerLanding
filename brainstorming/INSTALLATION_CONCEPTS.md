# FlowLayer Installation Concepts

## Core framing

FlowLayer should be presented as a speculative installation from a future where nobody drives manually anymore.

The project is not just:

- a driving simulator
- a route planner
- an AI assistant

It is a reconstruction of a lost behavior:

- steering
- traffic
- making wrong turns
- choosing the long way home
- assigning emotion to roads

That framing is stronger for a year-end show because it turns the experience into:

- a story
- a ritual
- a performance

## Two-screen format

## 1. Outside screen

Purpose:

- attract people in line
- explain the fiction
- make the installation legible without anyone touching it

What it should show:

- title treatment
- a short museum-style premise
- queue or "Now Driving" state
- live observer mode or route archive view
- generated "field notes" about the current participant

Examples:

- `Manual Driving Reconstruction Unit`
- `Driving, Before Automation`
- `Recovered Commuting Rituals`
- `Mobility Archive: Human Navigation`

## 2. Inside screen

Purpose:

- immersion
- route and vibe context
- AI guidance
- physical driving

What it should not do:

- explain too much lore
- duplicate the outside screen

## Concept directions

## 1. Museum of Lost Behaviors

Tone:

- calm
- elegant
- eerie
- institutional

Visual language:

- dark steel blues
- soft cyan
- muted amber
- precise typography
- archival overlays

Outside screen:

- "Today’s reconstruction"
- "Observed driving behaviors"
- "Archive notes"

Why it works:

- easiest for a year-end show audience to understand quickly
- feels smart instead of gimmicky

## 2. Traffic Archaeology

Tone:

- slightly stranger
- more poetic
- more systems-oriented

Premise:

- traffic jams, scenic drives, and commutes are treated as excavated social rituals

Outside screen language:

- `Recovered Pattern`
- `Commuter Drift`
- `Scenic Deviation`
- `Route Hesitation`

Why it works:

- gives the AI a role as a curator or analyst

## 3. Driving as Performance

Tone:

- theatrical
- public
- performative

Premise:

- the person in the chair is not just playing
- they are performing a reconstructed human behavior for an audience

Outside screen:

- live session title
- current route
- current emotional driving signature
- observer camera or map

Why it works:

- perfect for line-based installations
- people waiting still feel involved

## 4. Last Manual Driver

Tone:

- more cinematic
- more character-driven

Premise:

- the participant becomes a rare future-era person learning to manually drive

Why it works:

- stronger emotional hook
- easier to explain to a general audience

Risk:

- can become too narrative and less installation-like if overdone

## 5. Archive of the Long Way Home

Tone:

- emotional
- nostalgic
- reflective

Premise:

- the installation focuses less on efficiency and more on why humans chose routes for feeling

This supports:

- scenic routes
- wrong turns
- detours
- "chill" and "adventure" vibes

Why it works:

- aligns extremely well with FlowLayer’s route-vibe concept

## Best recommendation

The strongest path is a blend of:

- `Museum of Lost Behaviors`
- `Driving as Performance`
- `Archive of the Long Way Home`

That gives you:

- a clear fiction
- public-facing spectacle
- emotional route logic

## Outside screen ideas

## Observer mode

Show:

- overhead route trace
- destination
- current driving style
- AI curator commentary

Examples:

- `Subject favors scenic deviation over direct arrival.`
- `Manual steering corrections increasing near intersections.`
- `Emotional route preference: reflective.`

## Queue mode

Show:

- `Now Driving`
- `Next Participant`
- `Estimated Wait`
- title card and short premise

## Archive mode

Show:

- previous session summaries
- route cards
- museum captions

Examples:

- `Participant 014 chose the waterfront detour.`
- `Observed aversion to direct arterial roads.`
- `High steering confidence, low navigational patience.`

## Inside screen ideas

The simulator should feel:

- leaner
- more cinematic
- more physical

Good additions:

- route preview before start
- stronger destination framing
- AI assistant that feels like a curator, not a chatbot
- post-run summary card

## AI roles that actually make sense

## 1. Curator

Explains the installation and comments on behavior.

## 2. Archivist

Logs each drive like a recovered human ritual.

## 3. Route Psychologist

Interprets route choices as emotional or cultural behavior.

## 4. Mobility Historian

Frames traffic, parking, commuting, and detours as obsolete habits.

## Strong copy lines

- `In the future, nobody drives manually anymore.`
- `FlowLayer reconstructs what road-based decision making once felt like.`
- `Visitors are invited to perform a lost mobility ritual.`
- `Commuting was once physical, emotional, and inefficient.`
- `Humans once chose routes for reasons other than speed.`

## End-of-session outputs

Each participant should leave behind:

- a route summary
- a behavior classification
- a generated archive card

Possible labels:

- `Cautious Commuter`
- `Scenic Drifter`
- `Restless Navigator`
- `Late-Night Wanderer`
- `Shortcut Hunter`

## Physical staging ideas

- wheel and chair should feel ceremonial, almost like stepping into an old machine
- outside screen should be readable from across the room
- lighting should make the station feel like an exhibit, not a student demo table
- use sound sparingly and intentionally

## Best next design move

Design the outside screen first.

Why:

- it will define the tone of the whole installation
- it is what most people will see before they touch anything
- it forces the story to become clear
